Editorial Standards

How claims should be handled.

Crypto readers are often harmed by speed without verification. These rules are designed to make the site useful after the headline fades.

01

Primary sources first.

Use official filings, regulator pages, project documentation, GitHub repositories, issuer pages, data dashboards, and original statements whenever possible.

02

Separate fact from interpretation.

A price move, a filing, a token listing, and a social claim are different evidence types. They should not be treated as equal proof.

03

Label uncertainty.

If a story is developing, the page should say what is known, what is not known, and what source would confirm the next step.

04

Route news into evergreen context.

Fresh coverage should link to guides on ETFs, wallets, RWA, stablecoins, scams, market data, and policy so readers can keep learning.

05

No financial advice.

Articles and tools are educational. They should not tell readers what to buy, sell, hold, or trade.

06

Corrections stay visible.

When a material claim is wrong, unclear, outdated, or missing a better source, the page should be updated and the stronger source should be added.
