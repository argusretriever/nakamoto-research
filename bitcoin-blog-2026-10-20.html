Retrospective exercise for 20 October 2026

A vacancy is a position, not an unemployed person

Learn why a labour-market story needs the measure, population, and period attached to every number.

Written and published 24 October 2026. Archive study date: 20 October 2026. This page does not claim it was available on the earlier date.

Use the published bulletin

Original source. The ONS October 2026 labour-market overview estimates 691,000 vacancies for July–September, down 21,000 from April–June. Its unemployment rate is 4.7% for people aged 16 and over in June–August. These estimates cover different units and overlapping but non-identical periods.

Label the denominator

Write “vacant positions” beside the first number and “share of the economically active population aged 16+” beside the unemployment rate. Do not subtract one from the other or describe a one-to-one matching problem. A vacancy can be filled by someone who already has a job, and an unemployed person may not match its location or requirements.

Note the uncertainty

The bulletin calls early payroll estimates provisional and discusses data quality. Before a market or policy inference, record which series is being revised and which observation is a survey estimate. The companion 21 October exercise explores the period differences in more detail.
