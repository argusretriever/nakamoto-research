Basis points, percentages and a rate-change evidence log

Published 7 November 2026

Practise describing a policy-rate change without confusing levels, percentage points and relative changes.

Work through a hypothetical example

Suppose a rate moves from 3% to 3.25%. The new level is 3.25%; the increase is 0.25 percentage points, or 25 basis points. The relative increase is 0.25 ÷ 3 × 100, approximately 8.33%. These are different descriptions of the same hypothetical move.

Build the evidence log

  1. Copy the actual rate level and change from the linked official decision.
  2. Write the majority vote and the alternative proposal in separate cells.
  3. Record the source publication date and your check date.
  4. Label any assumed future rate as a scenario, with a horizon and a reason it could fail.

Check your conclusion

Ask whether your wording confuses a percentage change with a percentage-point change, whether dissent is explained, and whether a product rate has been mistaken for the policy rate. Use the evidence worksheet for the source log.
