ETF due diligence

ETF knowledge that prevents expensive mistakes

An ETF is not just a ticker. It is a wrapper, index methodology, cost structure, liquidity profile, tax object, and portfolio role.

Know the index

The benchmark decides what the ETF owns and how holdings are weighted.

Example: market-cap, equal-weight, factor, dividend, and sector indexes behave differently.

  • Mistake: assuming similar names mean similar exposure.
Compare total ownership cost

Expense ratio matters, but so do spreads, tracking difference, taxes, and trading behavior.

Example: a low-fee ETF with poor liquidity can still be costly for frequent traders.

  • Mistake: choosing only the lowest expense ratio.
Check portfolio overlap

Two ETFs can duplicate the same stocks and make a portfolio look diversified when it is not.

Example: total market plus large-cap growth can double up mega-cap exposure.

  • Mistake: owning many funds without mapping holdings.
