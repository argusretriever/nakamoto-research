Foundation curriculum

Lessons that turn concepts into investor judgment.

Each lesson gives the reader a definition, reason, exercise, mistake to avoid, and next page.

Lesson 1

Build an emergency buffer before investing aggressively.

Why: cash reserves prevent forced selling when life interrupts the plan. Exercise: estimate 3-6 months of core expenses and decide where that cash should sit. Mistake: investing money needed soon because markets look attractive.

Goal-based investing
Lesson 2

Understand accounts before choosing tickers.

Why: taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts change how returns are taxed and accessed. Exercise: list each account, purpose, time horizon, contribution rules, and withdrawal constraints. Mistake: placing every fund in every account without a tax-location plan.

Resource library
Lesson 3

Choose allocation before choosing funds.

Why: allocation is the portfolio's risk engine. Exercise: write target weights and rebalance bands before selecting ETFs. Mistake: owning many funds that secretly duplicate the same exposure.

Model portfolios
Lesson 4

Read market news as a review prompt.

Why: news should inform assumptions, not trigger reflex trades. Exercise: classify each headline as action, review, save, or ignore. Mistake: treating every CPI, Fed, or earnings headline as urgent.

Sample briefing
Lesson 5

Write a portfolio policy statement.

Why: written rules make behavior easier under stress. Exercise: define goals, allocation, rebalancing, contributions, withdrawals, and review cadence. Mistake: relying on memory during volatile markets.

Download templates
Lesson 6

Review progress on a calendar, not a feeling.

Why: regular reviews help investors improve without overreacting. Exercise: schedule monthly learning, quarterly portfolio review, and annual plan review. Mistake: checking constantly but reviewing nothing systematically.
