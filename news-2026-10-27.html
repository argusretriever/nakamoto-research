Bank of England speech maps climate shocks to inflation and credit

James Talbot’s 23 October speech explains why droughts, floods, and heatwaves can reach prices and output through different channels. It is analysis, not a new Bank Rate decision.

Published 27 October 2026 as a retrospective study for the 27 October archive slot. Original source: Bank of England speech by James Talbot, published 23 October 2026. The source predates this NAKAMOTO publication.

Different shocks, different channels

In the Bank of England speech, Talbot describes damage to production, labour supply, transport, supply chains, confidence, and credit. A drought may reduce farm output and lift food prices; a flood can also weaken demand. The direction of inflation depends on the channel, so a physical event is not automatically one market signal.

Direct and second-round effects

The speech distinguishes an initial relative-price effect from wider pass-through and later wage or expectation effects. It argues that central banks should assess magnitude, persistence, and propagation before judging policy. This framework does not prescribe a specific Bank Rate move.

Insurance and resilience

Talbot discusses how insurance gaps and weaker buffers can deepen the effect of repeated shocks on households, collateral, lenders, and public finances. The speech is a risk framework; it does not establish an individual mortgage premium, forecast, or investment return.
