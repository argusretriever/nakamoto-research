Safety

Stay current first

Protect minimum payments and essential bills before extra payoff. New late fees can undo progress.

Clarity

List every debt

Include balance, APR, minimum, due date, lender, and emotional pressure. Hidden debts keep control vague.

Strategy

Choose a method

Avalanche targets highest APR first. Snowball targets smallest balance first. The best method is the one you can keep using.

Prevention

Build a buffer

A starter emergency fund helps stop the debt plan from breaking when a surprise cost appears.

Example

Why APR matters

High-interest credit card debt can grow quickly when only minimums are paid. Extra principal payments have the most impact when they reduce the balance that interest is calculated on.

Mindset

Shame is not a strategy

A clear plan works better than guilt. The first win may be opening statements, listing debts, or setting autopay for minimums.

Is debt snowball or avalanche better?

Avalanche can reduce interest cost by targeting the highest APR first. Snowball can improve motivation by paying smaller balances first. Behavior matters because an abandoned plan saves nothing.

Should I consolidate debt?

Consolidation may help if it lowers cost, simplifies payments, and does not create new spending room that becomes more debt. Read terms carefully.

What if a debt collector contacts me?

Document the contact, ask for validation if needed, and understand your rights. Official consumer protection resources can help explain debt collection rules.
