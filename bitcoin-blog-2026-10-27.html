Trace a physical shock before making a market claim

Use a source-to-channel map to separate a physical event, its economic transmission, and any policy inference.

Published 27 October 2026 as a retrospective study for the 27 October archive slot. Original source: Bank of England speech by James Talbot, published 23 October 2026. The source predates this NAKAMOTO publication.

Draw the chain

Start with one example in the Bank speech, such as drought. Write: physical event → production or logistics effect → prices and output → possible wider pass-through. At each arrow, note what evidence would confirm the link and how long it could last.

Keep document status visible

The source is an official speech by a Bank executive, not Monetary Policy Committee minutes or a rate announcement. A framework for assessing shocks cannot be relabelled as a policy decision.

Apply a reader safety check

If a headline jumps from an extreme-weather event to a Bitcoin, stock, or mortgage prediction, ask for a dated primary source, a measured transmission path, and a comparison period. If those are absent, record the claim as unverified.

Check the source and continue

Read the original document for definitions, caveats, and later revisions. Continue with the companion source review, the 27 October source pack, or the dated archive.
