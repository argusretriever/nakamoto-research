Do not add monthly percentages mechanically

Use multiplication when a change applies to a new base.

Archive slot: 27 August 2026
Published 13 September 2026. This educational retrospective fills an archive gap. It is not a report of market conditions on the archive date.

Source foundation

CPI measures changes in consumer prices; it is not an individual household budget. BLS: CPI questions and answers.

A worked example

A hypothetical index grows 1 percent for two consecutive months. Starting at 100, it reaches 101 and then 102.01. The combined gain is 2.01 percent. Adding the two monthly rates gives a close approximation here, but it is not the exact compounded result.

Original hypothetical example. Amounts, people, events, and scenarios above are invented for learning; they are not historical observations or forecasts.

What to take from it

Label approximations and distinguish them from exact calculations.

Try it on paper

Compute two consecutive hypothetical declines of 1 percent.

Keep the assumptions beside your answer. Check the result before applying the idea to a different situation.
