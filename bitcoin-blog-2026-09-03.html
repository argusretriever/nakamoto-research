Separate willingness from capacity to take risk

Write down emotional comfort and financial constraints independently.

Source foundation

Risk tolerance and the time available before spending matter when considering investments. Investor.gov: gauge your risk tolerance.

A worked example

A fictional investor says a large price swing feels tolerable but needs the money for a fixed bill in two months. The worksheet records both statements instead of allowing enthusiasm to erase the deadline. Another person might have a long horizon yet find volatility distressing.

What to take from it

Different constraints can point in different directions and deserve explicit discussion.

Try it on paper

Create separate columns for deadlines, cash needs, and emotional reactions.

Keep the assumptions beside your answer. Check the result before applying the idea to a different situation.
