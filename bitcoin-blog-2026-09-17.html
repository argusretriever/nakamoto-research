Keep published facts and pending releases on separate lines

A September 17 research note on preserving a clean boundary between verified releases, pending events, and Bitcoin observations.

Published 17 September 2026.

Use explicit status labels

At the 08:29 London check, the Federal Reserve decision and the UK August inflation bulletin were published. The Bank of England decision and US housing release were still pending on their official pages. A good daily note says “published,” “pending,” or “not retrieved”; it does not fill a gap with an expectation.

Record the UK inflation result precisely

The Office for National Statistics reported August CPI inflation of 3.1% year over year, up from 2.9%, and CPIH of 3.3%, up from 3.1%. Transport, particularly motor fuels, made the largest upward contribution. These figures describe the release; they do not by themselves establish a Bitcoin signal.

Timestamp the Bitcoin observation

The dated source check recorded a Bitcoin quote of $76,482. A quote without its retrieval time and source is not a durable research observation. Preserve both, and avoid presenting one snapshot as a daily high, low, close, or fair value.

Practice

Open today’s official-source checklist and create three rows: verified release, pending release, and market observation. Add source and retrieval-time columns. Write any interpretation in a separate paragraph only after every factual row has a status.
