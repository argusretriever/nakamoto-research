01 Decision Trees

Turn confusion into next steps.

Decision trees help readers choose a path without pretending every user has the same goal or risk tolerance.

New beginner

Learn first, buy later.

If a reader cannot explain seed phrases, exchange custody, and irreversible transactions, the safest next step is education and a small test amount, not a large purchase.

ETF or BTC?

Exposure versus ownership.

If a reader wants brokerage simplicity, an ETF may fit. If they want bearer ownership and withdrawal control, they need self-custody skills.

Hot wallet?

Convenience only.

Use hot wallets for learning and small spending. Do not treat a phone wallet like a vault for life savings.

Hardware wallet?

Meaningful savings.

When the amount becomes emotionally or financially meaningful, offline signing and seed backup discipline become more important.

Multisig?

Large balances or shared control.

Use multisig when single-key loss is unacceptable, but only if recovery, documentation, and heirs are planned.

Business treasury?

Policy before purchase.

A business should define board approval, custody roles, accounting, insurance, liquidity, and disclosure before buying BTC.

02 Red Flag Library

Warning signs before money moves.

Seed phrase request

Any site, person, wallet support account, giveaway, or recovery service asking for seed words is a critical danger signal.

Guaranteed yield

Promises of safe high returns, cloud mining profits, or risk-free doubling are usually designed to bypass judgment.

Fake urgency

Scams push deadlines: act now, verify now, send now, unlock now. Real security work slows down.

Impersonation

Fake exchange staff, fake wallet support, fake founders, and fake recovery agents often appear after users post about problems.

Download risk

Ads, cloned websites, typo domains, and unofficial app stores can distribute fake wallets or clipboard malware.

Withdrawal trap

If a platform demands more deposits, taxes, unlock fees, or verification payments before withdrawal, treat it as likely fraud.

03 Loss Calculator

Loss is not only price going down.

A useful calculator should show preventable losses, not just investment returns.

Loss TypeWhat It MeansHow To Reduce It
SpreadThe difference between quoted market price and actual buy/sell execution.Compare total price, not just stated fee.
Network feeBitcoin transaction cost paid to miners.Use fee estimates, avoid urgent movement during congestion.
Exchange feeTrading, withdrawal, deposit, or card purchase cost.Use transparent fee schedules and test small withdrawals.
Tax dragRealized gains, income treatment, poor records, and avoidable short-term actions.Keep records and consult tax guidance before complex moves.
Custody lossLost seed, broken device, exchange failure, phishing, or family unable to recover.Use backups, hardware wallets, test recovery, and inheritance planning.
Scam lossFraudulent platforms, fake giveaways, recovery scams, malware, or impersonation.Never share seed words. Verify domains. Slow down before sending.

04 Scenario Simulator

Model assumptions instead of hype.

Adoption

Who holds Bitcoin?

Households, companies, ETFs, banks, miners, funds, and governments all create different demand patterns.

Inflation

Nominal price can mislead.

A higher BTC/USD price may reflect Bitcoin adoption, dollar weakness, or both.

Regulation

Gateway access matters.

Exchange rules, custody rules, tax treatment, and ETF access shape adoption speed.

Supply loss

Not all BTC moves.

Lost coins and long-term cold storage reduce liquid supply, which can amplify price moves.

05 Family Guide

Explain Bitcoin without exposing secrets.

Spouse

Focus on recovery roles.

Explain what exists, who to call, and what never to type online. Do not casually share seed phrases.

Parents

Use familiar comparisons.

Compare Bitcoin to digital bearer property, not a bank login. Emphasize scams and irreversible payments.

Children

Teach privacy early.

Do not brag about holdings. Do not click giveaway links. Do not reveal family custody details.

Business co-owner

Use policy, not vibes.

Define approvals, custody roles, signing authority, accounting, and emergency procedures.

06 Estate Binder

Inheritance planning without creating a treasure map.

Inventory

List wallet type, custodian type, device location category, and whether multisig is used. Avoid writing full seed words in the binder.

People

Identify executor, attorney, trusted technical helper, and family contact. Separate roles when possible.

Instructions

Explain what not to do: do not reset devices, do not enter seed words online, do not answer random support messages.

Verification

Include watch-only addresses or account statements where appropriate so heirs can verify existence without spending authority.

Legal layer

Coordinate with estate documents, jurisdiction rules, taxes, and beneficiary planning.

Review cycle

Review annually and after marriage, divorce, relocation, major purchase, or custody change.

08 Neutral Reviews

Review products by criteria, not hype.

Wallets

Key control first.

Review open-source status, backup method, hardware support, coin control, privacy, recovery clarity, and official download path.

Exchanges

Withdrawal quality matters.

Review country support, fees, spread, withdrawal speed, proof of reserves, security controls, and support history.

Hardware wallets

Supply-chain risk matters.

Review secure element design, firmware process, screen verification, seed entry flow, and recovery documentation.

Books and apps

Educational honesty matters.

Review whether material separates facts from opinion, updates claims, cites sources, and avoids hype.

09 Historical Mistakes

Past failures are curriculum.

Mt. Gox

Exchange custody risk.

Leaving coins on a platform creates counterparty risk. A balance screen is not the same as holding keys.

FTX

Governance and asset segregation.

Brand confidence, famous backers, and slick interfaces do not replace audited controls and withdrawal discipline.

Celsius / BlockFi

Yield is not savings.

Lending platforms can hide credit, liquidity, rehypothecation, and legal risks behind simple yield language.

Quadriga

Key-person risk.

If one person controls access or records, customers may be exposed to extreme operational failure.

Lost hard drives

Backups beat memory.

Self-custody requires recovery planning. A device is not the asset; the keys are the asset.

Seed leaks

Digital copies spread.

Photos, cloud notes, printers, compromised computers, and messaging apps can turn a backup into a leak.

10 What Changed?

Before and after explainers.

Before ETF

Access was harder.

Many investors needed exchanges or trusts. After spot ETFs, brokerage access became easier but self-custody was not replaced.

Before halving

Issuance was higher.

After each halving, miner revenue composition changes and fees become more important over time.

Before self-custody

The exchange is the risk center.

After withdrawal, the user controls keys and becomes responsible for backup, privacy, and inheritance.

Before regulation

Access may be unclear.

After formal rules, compliance can improve access while also increasing reporting and restrictions.

11 Confidence Score

Am I ready?

Ready to learn

You can explain what Bitcoin is, why keys matter, and why transactions are irreversible.

Ready to buy small

You understand exchange fees, spread, tax records, and test withdrawals.

Ready for self-custody

You can create an offline backup, verify addresses, send a test transaction, and recover a wallet.

Ready to hold long term

You have a plan for volatility, taxes, custody review, inheritance, and avoiding panic decisions.

12 Source Notes

Show readers what was verified.

Claim

What the article says.

Example: a company bought BTC, a regulator changed policy, or an exchange reported proof of reserves.

Evidence

Where the claim came from.

Prefer filings, official statements, primary documents, court records, or reputable reporting with named sources.

Uncertainty

What is not proven.

Separate confirmed facts from interpretation, estimates, anonymous claims, or early reports.

14 Mistake Recovery

What to do when something goes wrong.

Wrong address

Do not assume recovery.

Bitcoin transactions are generally irreversible. Contact the recipient only if known; beware recovery scams.

Low fee

Check replace or accelerate options.

Some wallets support replace-by-fee or child-pays-for-parent. Do not rebroadcast random advice without understanding it.

Lost device

Use the backup, not panic.

A lost hardware wallet is recoverable if the seed backup is intact and private.

Exposed seed

Move funds immediately.

Create a new secure wallet and transfer funds if possible. Treat the old seed as permanently compromised.

Scam link clicked

Disconnect and review.

Do not enter more information. Secure accounts, change passwords, revoke sessions where relevant, and scan devices.

Exchange frozen

Document everything.

Save transaction IDs, account notices, support messages, dates, and legal documents before taking next steps.

15 Life Events

Bitcoin planning changes when life changes.

Marriage

Clarify ownership, beneficiary intent, custody knowledge, and what a spouse needs to know in an emergency.

Divorce

Get legal advice, preserve records, avoid hiding assets, and understand tax and disclosure duties.

Death

Inheritance planning should let heirs recover funds without exposing seed words casually.

Moving country

Review tax residency, exchange access, reporting rules, customs issues, and local crypto restrictions.

Tax season

Collect buys, sells, transfers, fees, income, mining, gifts, donations, and exchange statements.

Retirement

Review volatility tolerance, liquidity needs, inheritance, tax planning, and custody simplicity.
