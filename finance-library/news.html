News literacy

How to read market news without overreacting

Market news is useful when it updates an assumption, source, or risk. It is dangerous when it becomes a reflex to trade.

Classify every headline

Use four labels: action, review, save, or ignore. Most news belongs in review or ignore, not action.

Example: CPI above expectations may trigger a review of bond duration and cash yield, not an immediate portfolio overhaul.

  • Mistake: treating each headline as urgent.
Demand a source link

A useful news item should point to the release, filing, speech, calendar, or dataset behind the claim.

Example: use BLS for CPI, BEA for GDP, Federal Reserve for FOMC, SEC EDGAR for filings.

  • Mistake: relying on unsourced commentary.
Connect to portfolio impact

Ask which assumption changed: inflation, earnings, rates, valuation, liquidity, or risk tolerance.

Example: a rate move matters differently for cash, long bonds, growth stocks, and mortgage-sensitive sectors.

  • Mistake: reading news without a decision framework.
