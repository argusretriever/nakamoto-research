Market framework

How to understand market conditions

A market view should combine valuation, earnings, rates, liquidity, breadth, sentiment, and risk instead of relying on one chart.

Valuation is context, not timing

Expensive markets can keep rising and cheap markets can stay cheap. Use valuation to set expectations, not exact entry points.

Example: compare valuation with earnings growth and rates.

  • Mistake: turning valuation into a short-term signal.
Breadth shows participation

Market breadth asks whether gains are broad or concentrated in a few names.

Example: index strength with weak breadth can mean concentration risk is rising.

  • Mistake: assuming the index tells the whole story.
Rates affect discount rates

Rates influence bonds, cash returns, equity multiples, and debt-heavy companies.

Example: higher long yields pressure long-duration assets more than short-duration cash.

  • Mistake: ignoring duration.
