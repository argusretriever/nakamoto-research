How NAKAMOTO Finance Library earns trust before it asks for attention.

These standards guide sourcing, corrections, updates, conflicts, educational framing, and the way every investing page explains risk.

Rules

Every article should make the reader more disciplined.

NAKAMOTO Finance Library content is designed to inform decisions without pretending that a public article knows a reader's full situation.

Sourcing

Use primary sources when possible.

Market data, economic releases, filings, ETF details, and regulatory information should link to official or high-authority sources.

Corrections

Fix errors clearly.

Material corrections should be updated on the page with a note explaining what changed and when.

Conflicts

Disclose incentives.

Educational content should identify when commercial, affiliate, sponsorship, or portfolio conflicts could affect perception.

Advice boundary

Keep education separate from advice.

NAKAMOTO Finance Library explains concepts, tools, and frameworks. It does not provide individualized investment, legal, or tax advice.

AI assistance

Use AI as support, not authority.

AI-assisted drafts must still be checked for accuracy, sources, tone, and investor safety before publication.

Updates

Review evergreen content.

Pages that can become outdated should show review dates, source refreshes, and links to current market context.

Required page elementsClear headline, summary, sources, limitations, related content, next step, and disclosure path.
Risk languageExplain uncertainty, assumptions, time horizon, and what could make the conclusion wrong.
Reader outcomeEach page should help a visitor understand, compare, decide what to review, or know what to ignore.
EscalationQuestions, corrections, and source concerns should route to the contact page.
