Beginner Safety

Bitcoin mistakes beginners make.

The most common mistakes are buying before understanding custody, leaving too much on an exchange, trusting social media offers, saving seed words online, and sending large test transactions without practice.

  • Start small and learn the workflow.
  • Never share seed words or private keys.
  • Test withdrawals and recovery before storing serious money.

Seed Backup

Seed phrase backup planner.

A seed phrase backup should be offline, private, durable, and recoverable by the right person at the right time. Paper is simple but fragile. Metal is more durable but still needs careful storage.

  • Do not photograph or upload seed words.
  • Consider fire, water, theft, and heirs.
  • Document what wallet type the backup belongs to.

Hardware Wallets

Hardware wallet comparison.

Hardware wallets should be compared by ease of setup, screen clarity, Bitcoin-only support, firmware transparency, backup flow, multisig support, and recovery documentation.

  • Beginners should prioritize clear setup and recovery.
  • Advanced users may prefer air-gapped signing or multisig support.
  • Always buy from official sources where possible.

Tax

Bitcoin tax by country.

Bitcoin tax treatment differs by country and activity. Holding, selling, trading, mining, receiving income, donating, gifting, or spending can each create different reporting duties.

  • Track cost basis from the beginning.
  • Separate transfers from taxable disposals.
  • Verify official rules in your country.

Family

Bitcoin for parents and family.

Family explanations should avoid trading language. Start with saving, digital ownership, scams, passwords, and why losing a backup can be permanent.

  • Use simple examples, not market predictions.
  • Explain that Bitcoin transactions cannot be reversed.
  • Make safety the first lesson.

Business

Bitcoin for business owners.

Business owners need a practical checklist before accepting or holding Bitcoin: custody, accounting, tax, treasury policy, payment operations, and who can approve transfers.

  • Separate operating cash from long-term reserves.
  • Define who can move funds and under what conditions.
  • Get accounting and legal review before meaningful exposure.

Privacy

Bitcoin privacy guide.

Bitcoin is public and pseudonymous, not fully anonymous. Privacy means reducing unnecessary exposure while staying lawful and avoiding behavior that creates extra risk.

  • Do not reuse addresses when avoidable.
  • Understand that exchange withdrawals may link identity to addresses.
  • Privacy tools can have legal and compliance implications.

Nodes

Bitcoin node guide.

A Bitcoin node checks the rules independently. It helps users verify transactions, enforce supply rules, and avoid relying completely on third-party servers.

  • Most beginners do not need a node on day one.
  • Nodes improve verification and privacy when used correctly.
  • Node packages make setup easier but still require maintenance.

Lightning

Lightning Network beginner guide.

Lightning is a payment network built around Bitcoin that can make small payments faster and cheaper, but it introduces channel, liquidity, routing, and wallet trade-offs.

  • Use Lightning for small payments, not long-term savings.
  • Understand custodial versus self-custodial Lightning wallets.
  • Fees and reliability depend on routing and liquidity.

Halving

Bitcoin halving page.

A halving reduces the new bitcoin subsidy every 210,000 blocks. It affects miner revenue and the issuance schedule, but it does not guarantee price increases.

  • Current subsidy after the 2024 halving is 3.125 BTC per block.
  • The next halving is expected around 2028 at block 1,050,000.
  • Fees become more important as the subsidy declines.

Emergency

Bitcoin emergency page.

If a wallet may be compromised, the user should stop sharing information, disconnect suspicious apps, avoid typing seed words, move funds only from a clean setup if they understand the process, and seek qualified help for large amounts.

  • If someone has your seed phrase, assume the funds are at risk.
  • Do not enter seed words into a website or support chat.
  • For large balances, slow down and use expert help before making mistakes worse.
