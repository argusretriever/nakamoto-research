How to read

Start with the question.

Every visual should answer one learning question: what changed, where is value, who may be trapped, or what invalidates the idea?

How to practice

Redraw from memory.

After studying a diagram, learners should cover the labels and rebuild the concept. This turns visual recognition into active recall.

How to review

Compare with failure cases.

Each diagram needs a paired non-example so learners understand when the concept does not apply.

Graphic communication standards

Every visual has to earn its place.

These rules convert graphic design principles into NAKAMOTO Academy production standards for lessons, diagrams, charts, tables, and study tools.

01 / Foundation

Define the visual job

Every asset must answer one clear question: identify a pattern, compare two states, explain a process, show risk, or support recall.

  • Best practice: one main idea per graphic.
  • Mistake to avoid: decorative charts with no learning question.
02 / Composition

Use hierarchy before detail

Focal point, scale, contrast, spacing, and alignment must tell the learner where to look first, second, and third.

  • Best practice: title, visual, label, takeaway.
  • Mistake to avoid: equal-weight labels everywhere.
03 / Typography

Make labels scannable

Use short labels, consistent case, generous line-height, and clear type roles for headings, tags, values, and warnings.

  • Best practice: labels explain, not decorate.
  • Mistake to avoid: tiny annotation text on dense visuals.
04 / Color

Assign colors a job

Green means constructive or bullish context, coral means risk or bearish pressure, amber means caution, blue means explanation, and dark ink means authority.

  • Best practice: color plus text label.
  • Mistake to avoid: relying on color alone.
05 / Symbols

Keep marks consistent

Icons, dots, lines, zones, candles, tags, and profile bars must use repeated shapes so learners build visual memory across pages.

  • Best practice: one symbol family per concept family.
  • Mistake to avoid: changing visual metaphors between lessons.
06 / Portfolio quality

Show process and failure

Each major visual should include the concept, the non-example, the beginner mistake, the professional use, and a practice task.

  • Best practice: diagram plus drill.
  • Mistake to avoid: polished visuals without student action.

Editorial standard

Visual lessons must explain the concept, show when it fails, define risk, and avoid presenting patterns as guaranteed predictions.
