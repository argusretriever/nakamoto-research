Build a research evidence worksheet

Turn a headline into a record that you can check later.

Copy these fields into your notes

  1. Question: what are you trying to establish?
  2. Claim: one sentence, without a prediction hidden inside it.
  3. Original source: publisher, document title and direct link.
  4. Timing: source release date, reference period and retrieval time.
  5. Measure: unit, population, geography and price basis.
  6. Status: scheduled, provisional, revised or final as described by the publisher.
  7. Limits: what cannot be concluded from this evidence?
  8. Next check: what new document or correction would change your view?

Use the same discipline across subjects

For trading, record the event time before testing a market-reaction claim. For investing, keep company evidence separate from macro context. For household planning, distinguish an official rate from the actual terms of a contract. For Bitcoin, separate protocol facts from price forecasts.

A small worked example

A fictional release says a survey of 100 respondents found 20 reporting a change. Your record should say 20% of respondents, identify the survey period, and leave population-wide prevalence unknown. Repeating 20% as a national fact would exceed the evidence.
