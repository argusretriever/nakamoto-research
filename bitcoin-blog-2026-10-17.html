Retrospective exercise for 17 October 2026

Put bills on a calendar before comparing investments

A cash-flow calendar can reveal timing risk that an annual income total hides.

Written and published 24 October 2026. Archive study date: 17 October 2026. This page does not claim it was available on the earlier date.

Start with the toolkit

Original source. The CFPB Your Money, Your Goals toolkit includes a bill calendar, income tracker, spending tracker, and cash-flow budget. These are separate tools because income timing, bill due dates, and total monthly amounts answer different questions. The toolkit is educational, not a promise that one budget method fits everyone.

Draw the timeline

For a hypothetical month, put each expected income receipt and essential bill on its actual date. Calculate the balance after each event rather than comparing only month-end totals. A positive monthly surplus can still coexist with a shortfall before payday. Mark uncertain income separately from confirmed income.

Decide what is available to risk

Only after the timing check should you compare a savings or investment goal with the cash buffer you need. If the exercise reveals a gap, the next step is to review bill timing or expenses, not to rely on a market return to cover a near-term obligation.
