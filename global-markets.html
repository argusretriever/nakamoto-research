Archived 12 June market map.

Bitcoin rebounded with risk assets as oil fell and geopolitical stress eased. The useful reader frame: Bitcoin is a monetary network, but its short-term price still trades inside a global liquidity and risk system.

Market Categories

What each market tells Bitcoin readers.

This desk gives readers a clear market framework, source links, and internal research paths before deeper live-data features are added.

Crypto

Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, XRP.

Track Bitcoin first, then major networks, ETF demand, custody, exchanges, stablecoins, and security incidents.

Read: crypto-news-today.html
Forex

Dollar and currency strength.

A strong dollar can pressure risk assets. A weaker safe-haven bid can support risk appetite.

Watch: dollar, EUR/USD, USD/JPY
Stocks

Risk appetite and earnings.

Technology and high-growth stocks often show whether investors are comfortable taking risk.

Watch: S&P 500, Nasdaq, Dow
Global Stocks

Regional confirmation.

Asia, Europe, and U.S. markets together show whether a move is local or global.

Watch: Americas, Europe, Asia-Pacific
Indices

Market breadth.

Index behavior helps readers separate broad recovery from a narrow technology-led bounce.

Watch: Nasdaq, Russell 2000, DAX, Nikkei
Commodities

Oil, gold, and inflation pressure.

Oil affects inflation expectations. Gold remains the classic comparison for Bitcoin as a scarce asset.

Read: comparison-hub.html
ETFs

Access and product design.

Spot ETFs, futures ETFs, and income ETFs create different types of Bitcoin exposure.

Read: etf.html
Bonds

Rates and discount rates.

Bond yields influence liquidity, valuations, borrowing costs, and demand for risk assets.

Watch: U.S. Treasuries
Money Markets

Cash yield and liquidity.

Short-term rates and cash alternatives affect why investors hold cash, stocks, bonds, or Bitcoin.

Watch: T-bills, SOFR, repos
Real Estate

Rates and affordability.

Property markets show how higher rates affect households, credit, and long-cycle assets.

Watch: mortgage rates, REITs
Economy

GDP, inflation, jobs, confidence.

Economic data shapes central-bank expectations, liquidity, and risk appetite.
