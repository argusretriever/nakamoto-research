Check the unit and statistical status before building a sector comparison

Retrospective archive slot: 2026-11-02. Written 3 November 2026. Published 3 November 2026.

Original source: Office for National Statistics. 30 October 2026. Verified 3 November 2026.

Good research starts with a claim that another reader can trace and check.

Your task

Choose a sector and inspect the workbook headings before copying a value. Record the measure, unit, price basis, geography, observation year and statistical status. Compare only rows with compatible definitions.

Work through an example

For a hypothetical shop, sales of 100 and purchased inputs of 70 leave 30 before other accounting adjustments. Calling all 100 value added would count the inputs again. This example is illustrative, not an ONS estimate.

Write a checkable conclusion

  1. State exactly what your chosen row measures.
  2. Add its reference period, units and source link.
  3. Write one alternative explanation or limitation.
  4. Name the evidence that would change your conclusion.

Keep an empty field when the source does not supply an answer. A blank with a reason is more useful than an invented estimate.

Review your work

Could a reader reproduce the comparison using your notes? If a definition or period changed, explain that before drawing a conclusion. Use the evidence worksheet to organise the result.
