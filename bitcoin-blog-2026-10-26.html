Make an earnings comparison without switching populations

A headline about full-time pay cannot silently become a claim about all jobs or one household.

Published 27 October 2026 as a retrospective study for the 26 October archive slot. Original source: ONS employee earnings in the UK: 2026, published 22 October 2026. This article was not published on 26 October.

Label the population

Write three separate rows from the ONS earnings bulletin: full-time median gross weekly earnings, all-employee annual growth, and part-time annual growth. Keep the April 2026 reference date on every row.

Check the denominator

Full-time median weekly earnings were £811.20, up 5.8% on the year. All-employee earnings grew 4.9%; part-time earnings grew 2.9%. These percentages describe different groups. A changing job mix can shift an aggregate even when no single person changes hours.

Use it for a money decision

For a household plan, compare your own net pay, hours, fixed costs, and emergency savings. Do not treat a national gross median as your take-home income or as proof of a personal pay rise.
