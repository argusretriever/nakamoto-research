Research playbooks

How to turn a topic into a complete research note.

These playbooks help NAKAMOTO Finance Library publish deeper content: thesis, evidence, source links, risks, examples, and reader next steps.

ETF playbook

Compare funds by role, not ticker popularity.

Research question: What job should this ETF do in the portfolio? Evidence: benchmark methodology, holdings, fee, spread, tracking difference, assets, turnover, distributions, and overlap. Output: fund role, alternatives, tradeoffs, and who should keep researching.

  • Source: issuer fund page, prospectus, SEC filings, index methodology.
  • Next step: ETF fee checklist.
Stock playbook

Build a thesis before looking for confirmation.

Research question: What must be true for this company to compound value? Evidence: revenue quality, margins, ROIC, free cash flow, balance sheet, capital allocation, competition, and valuation range. Output: thesis, risks, watchlist triggers, and position-sizing guardrails.

  • Source: 10-K, 10-Q, earnings call, segment data, industry data.
  • Next step: Thesis library.
Macro playbook

Translate data releases into assumption checks.

Research question: Which portfolio assumptions does this data update? Evidence: release date, headline measure, core measure, revisions, trend, market expectations, and historical context. Output: what changed, what did not, and which portfolio area deserves review.

  • Source: BLS, BEA, Federal Reserve, FRED, Treasury.
  • Next step: Economic calendar.
Portfolio playbook

Connect research to written rules.

Research question: Does this portfolio still match the investor's goals, horizon, risk capacity, and behavior needs? Evidence: allocation drift, concentration, fees, taxes, liquidity, duration, withdrawals, and contribution plan. Output: keep, rebalance, simplify, review, or learn more.

  • Source: account data, fund documents, policy statement, tax constraints.
  • Next step: Portfolio clinic.
Behavior playbook

Audit the decision, not just the asset.

Research question: Is the investor reacting to evidence or emotion? Evidence: written thesis, review date, risk rule, time horizon, opportunity cost, and mistake journal. Output: action, review, ignore, save, or ask a better question.

  • Source: investor policy, journal, checklist, and framework library.
  • Next step: Frameworks.
Publishing playbook

Every article needs a knowledge trail.

Research question: What should the reader understand or do after reading? Evidence: source links, definitions, examples, limitations, related content, and one next step. Output: a page that is useful today and still discoverable later.

  • Source: editorial standards and source policy.
  • Next step: Editorial standards.
