Research exercise · 21 October 2026

Do not put three labour measures on one timeline

The ONS labour-market bulletin published on 20 October contains survey, payroll, and vacancy measures. Their reference periods differ, and an early payroll estimate can change.

Published 21 October 2026. Source release: 20 October 2026.

Build a three-row evidence table

First row: the Labour Force Survey's employment and unemployment rates cover June–August 2026. Second row: the early September payroll estimate comes from HMRC administrative data and is provisional. Third row: the vacancy estimate covers July–September 2026. Write the population, data source, period, and revision status beside each row. The ONS bulletin provides these labels.

Read a change with its denominator

The ONS estimated a 75.3% employment rate for ages 16–64 and a 4.7% unemployment rate for ages 16 and over in June–August. The rates use different age groups and definitions; they are not two parts of a simple 100% total. The ONS also estimated 691,000 vacancies for July–September, down 21,000 from the previous quarter. A vacancy is a position, not a person.

Mark the provisional number

The September payroll estimate was little changed on the month but fell on the year in the ONS release. The ONS says early estimates are likely to be revised as more data arrive. In your table, mark that row “provisional”; do not convert it into a settled conclusion about the entire labour market.

Keep the market story separate

Write one sentence stating only what the ONS measured. In a second column, list what would be needed to discuss rates, a portfolio, or Bitcoin: a published policy decision, the relevant asset's own data, and a defined time horizon. Do not attach a price prediction to the labour bulletin alone. Continue with the source review, inflation-release method, or Research Foundation.

Educational research only. Recheck the ONS source for revisions before quoting figures or making a personal decision.
